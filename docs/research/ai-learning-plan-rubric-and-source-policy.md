# AI learning-plan rubric and trusted-source policy

Research note for the proposed Telegram learning-path generator (issue #28). The model should draft a plan for learner approval, not certify that it is educationally effective. The evidence below supports a **testable design rubric**; it does not establish a universal recipe or objective quality score.

## Evidence-informed plan rubric

| Check | Testable review criterion | Evidence / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Goal → outcomes | Convert the learner's goal into a small set of learner-centered, specific, observable outcomes (e.g. “explain,” “build,” “solve”); each outcome is assessable and feasible for the stated time, level, and context. Flag vague outcomes or missing constraints. | Cornell CTI advises clear, measurable, realistic outcomes aligned to course/program goals; its suggested outcome stem uses an action verb. These are design recommendations, not evidence that any specific generated objective will work. [Cornell: Defining learning outcomes](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/preparing-teach/designing-your-course/defining-learning-outcomes) |
| Prior knowledge and prerequisites | Ask what the learner already knows; represent prerequisite relationships explicitly, and distinguish confirmed knowledge from assumptions. Offer diagnostic questions or a bridge/remediation topic where uncertain. | Cornell recommends assessing prior knowledge to identify gaps and misconceptions and connect prior to new knowledge. This supports diagnosis, but does not supply an automatic universal prerequisite graph. [Cornell: Assessing prior knowledge](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/assessment-evaluation/assessing-prior-knowledge-addressing-learning-gaps) |
| Topic sequence and coverage | Topics collectively map to stated outcomes; prerequisite edges are acyclic; no outcome lacks a learning activity/resource or a way to check learning. Show why the ordering is proposed and let the learner reorder/edit it. | Cornell recommends curriculum alignment and consultation about sequencing; it notes curricula can have gaps or duplication. Whether a particular sequence is appropriate remains subject- and learner-dependent. [Cornell: Defining learning outcomes](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/preparing-teach/designing-your-course/defining-learning-outcomes) |
| Practice and review | Include retrieval opportunities (quizzes/questions), spaced review, and appropriate alternation of worked examples and independent problem solving where applicable; avoid making a plan only a list of readings. | The U.S. Department of Education What Works Clearinghouse practice guide recommends spacing learning, interleaving worked examples with problem-solving, using quizzes for retrieval, and delayed review. The guide covers content learning across several subjects; it is not a guarantee for every topic or learner. [IES/WWC: Organizing Instruction and Study](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) |
| Self-regulation and feedback | Include a lightweight plan/monitor/review loop: learner checks confidence or performance, identifies gaps, and adjusts next steps; specify what feedback or self-check informs adjustment. | EEF describes planning, monitoring, and evaluating as strategies for metacognition/self-regulation and says these approaches appear more effective when embedded in curriculum and subject lessons. This is contextual evidence, not a mandate for a fixed plan template. [EEF: Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/metacognition) |
| Effort estimates | Provide ranges and assumptions (weekly hours, existing knowledge, practice intensity); check that topic estimates sum consistently to the available schedule and label estimates as uncertain. | These sources support realistic outcomes and study planning, but do not establish reliable universal estimates of hours-to-mastery. Treat estimate accuracy as an empirical product question to measure against learner-reported/observed outcomes, not as model-certified fact. [Cornell CTI](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/preparing-teach/designing-your-course/defining-learning-outcomes), [IES/WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) |

### What software can validate vs. what needs judgment

**Deterministically testable:** required fields/schema and bounded lengths; a non-empty goal and outcomes; valid references between topics; prerequisite graph has no cycles; topic order is a valid topological order; each objective is linked to at least one topic and assessment/check; effort values are positive and totals fit stated schedule within an explicit tolerance; resource URL parses as HTTPS, has no userinfo, and its normalized hostname passes the configured allowlist; retrieval timestamp, HTTP response/status and redirect chain are recorded; duplicate links can be collapsed. These checks validate structure and policy, not learning value. HTTP/HTTPS syntax and origin concepts are specified in [RFC 9110, §§4.2–4.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110).

**Human review (learner approval and, for consequential/high-stakes plans, subject-matter review):** whether the goal was understood; level and breadth; correctness and completeness of topic coverage; whether prerequisites truly apply; whether activities/resources are pedagogically suitable and accessible; appropriateness of effort assumptions; whether the sequence fits this individual; and whether the sources are authoritative for the exact claims. A rule-based or LLM rubric can flag evidence and omissions for review, but cannot objectively certify educational quality. Approval must happen before the plan/resources become the learner's active path.

## Trusted-source discovery and URL controls

1. **Discover from a bounded catalog, not arbitrary model-supplied links.** Configure approved institutions/publishers and exact hostnames (optionally explicit subdomains), organized by subject and resource type. Prefer first-party educational institutions, standards bodies, government resources, and original scholarly records. A domain allowlist is a provenance/access policy, not proof that every page is correct or suitable. Allowlist updates are an explicit maintainer decision.
2. **Keep provenance per suggestion.** Store/display title, canonical URL, source host, author/publisher where available, publication or updated date, date checked, content type, and a short relevance rationale tied to a plan outcome. For scholarly material, Crossref's REST API exposes member-deposited bibliographic metadata and post-publication updates; metadata may be incomplete and is not an endorsement or quality assessment. [Crossref REST API](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/rest-api/)
3. **Validate before presenting and on click.** Parse URLs with a standard URL parser; permit HTTPS only (unless an explicit exception is reviewed); reject credentials/userinfo, IP-literal hosts, unexpected ports and malformed/obfuscated hosts; compare normalized hostname against exact allowlist entries (not substring matching). If redirects are followed, re-check every hop against scheme and allowlist, cap hops, and never fetch private/loopback/link-local destinations. This matters especially if the server fetches pages: OWASP identifies server-side request forgery as an attack surface. [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110), [OWASP: SSRF](https://owasp.org/www-community/attacks/Server_Side_Request_Forgery)
4. **Treat availability and freshness as snapshots.** Check links at discovery and immediately before approval/use where feasible; record checked-at and result, distinguish timeout/blocked/redirect/404 from “verified,” and re-check stale records on a defined TTL. Do not imply that a successful response means durable access, free access, or current content. For volatile topics, require a recent publication/update window or show the date and ask for review. A DOI/metadata record can help identify an item but does not establish that its full text is accessible or current.
5. **Optional security reputation check.** A URL reputation service such as Google Safe Browsing can add a risk signal; it is a supplemental deny/check, not a substitute for scheme/host validation, provenance review, or user approval. [Google Safe Browsing Lookup API](https://developers.google.com/safe-browsing/v4/lookup-api)

## Initial programming/CS catalog proposal

A practical initial exact-host allowlist (five hosts) for a first programming/CS slice:

| Exact hostname | Use / authority | Classification and limits |
|---|---|---|
| `docs.python.org` | Python language and library reference plus tutorial; the site identifies itself as the official Python documentation. [Python documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/) | Official documentation, not a complete programming curriculum. Tutorials target learners who already know how to program, so use selectively for true beginners. |
| `developer.mozilla.org` | HTML/CSS/JavaScript and web fundamentals; MDN Learn provides structured modules, challenges, and self-checks. [MDN Learn](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development) | Educational documentation, not the normative standards publisher. Community-created/open contribution content is reviewed but still needs page-level relevance and quality review; MDN notes its Learn material is created by the community. |
| `git-scm.com` | Git reference, tutorial, and user manual. The official Git documentation identifies itself as the manual for Git and points learners to the tutorial. [Git documentation](https://git-scm.com/docs/git) | Official project documentation, strong for tool operation, narrower than general software engineering or collaboration instruction. |
| `cs50.harvard.edu` | Harvard CS50x OpenCourseWare for introductory programming and CS concepts: computational thinking, algorithms, data structures, C, Python, SQL, and web technologies. [CS50x](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/) | University course, not language reference documentation. Course is free to take via OCW; assessments and feedback/credentials may involve separate services. Course material is instructor-produced, but community links/discussion and external platforms are not automatically covered by trusting this host. |
| `rust-lang.org` or `doc.rust-lang.org` | Optional Rust learning material and The Rust Programming Language book, linked from the Rust project’s Learn page. [Rust Learn](https://rust-lang.org/learn/) · [Rust Book](https://doc.rust-lang.org/book/) | Official project learning documentation; include only if Rust is in the initial supported language menu. The book acknowledges community contributions, so provenance does not eliminate page/content review. |

**Recommendation:** start with the first four hosts; add the two Rust hosts only when the product explicitly supports Rust (or treat them as one source family, while allowing both exact hosts). This provides one language reference, a broad web-learning resource, version-control docs, and a university CS course without turning the catalog into a general web search. For strict exact-host matching, list each hostname independently; do not allow `*.python.org`, `*.harvard.edu`, or other broad parent domains by implication.

**Coverage and trust boundaries:** this is an initial, deliberately incomplete catalog—not comprehensive across languages, operating systems, accessibility, security, databases, or advanced CS. MDN and Rust docs accept community contributions; university course pages can link to third-party platforms, forums, repositories, and media. An approved hostname establishes only a publisher/source boundary, not that every path, embedded link, learner level, or particular recommendation is correct or suitable. Keep to reviewed page URLs, distinguish documentation from courses, record exact final URL and redirect destination, and apply the existing provenance, freshness, URL, and learner-approval controls above. Reassess course editions and docs freshness periodically.

## Product decisions still open

- Which domains/catalogs are trusted initially, and who maintains the allowlist? Should third-party video platforms be allowed only through specific channels or playlists?
- Should the bot only present links, or server-fetch metadata/content? Fetching introduces SSRF, redirect, rate-limit, and privacy concerns; prefer metadata APIs or a tightly sandboxed fetcher if needed.
- What freshness TTL and topic-specific publication-age rules should apply, and how should unavailable links be labeled?
- Which learner constraints must be collected (current level, deadline, weekly hours, language/accessibility preferences), and is a subject-matter review required for any domains?
- What outcome measure will validate usefulness over time (learner approval/edit rate, link availability, completion, or demonstrated performance)? The sources do not provide a single correct metric or establish that approval predicts learning.
