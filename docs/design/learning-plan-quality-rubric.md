# Learning-plan quality review rubric

The generated plan includes an AI-written self-review for the criteria below. The notes cite topic/outcome IDs so the learner can inspect the rationale in Telegram. They are prompts for human review—not independent evidence that a plan is effective, and not model confidence scores.

| Criterion ID | Review question | Deterministic check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `goal_coverage` | Do the outcomes and topics address the learner's stated goal without unnecessary breadth? | Every outcome is linked to at least one topic; the review must cite all topic and outcome IDs. |
| `objective_assessment_alignment` | Does each outcome describe an observable capability, with an assessment that would reveal it? | Every outcome has a non-empty description and assessment; the review cites every outcome. |
| `topic_sequence` | Do topics build from prerequisites toward application or synthesis? | Prerequisites must refer to known topics, be acyclic, and appear earlier in the plan; the review cites every topic. |
| `practice_and_retrieval` | Does the plan include active practice that requires recalling or applying ideas, rather than only consuming material? | Every topic has a practice activity; the AI rationale must identify the supporting topics. |
| `scope_and_workload` | Is the proposed topic effort plausible within the learner's stated weekly budget and duration? | Topic estimates must be positive and their total must fit the available minutes; the review cites every topic. |

The deterministic checks validate structure, references, and declared constraints. They cannot determine whether the learner's goal is accurately understood, whether the assessments are educationally strong, or whether time estimates are realistic. The Telegram proposal labels quality notes as AI-generated and unverified, and resource links as suggestions rather than endorsements.

## Reference basis

- Carnegie Mellon University Eberly Center, [Course Content & Schedule](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/design/contentschedule.html): topic scope, sequencing toward greater complexity, practice, and realistic scheduling.
- Carnegie Mellon University Eberly Center, [Align Assessments, Objectives, Instructional Strategies](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/alignment.html): align observable learning objectives, assessments, and instructional activities.
- The Learning Scientists, [Six Strategies for Effective Learning](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2019/11/28-1): flexible use of spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice, concrete examples, elaboration, and dual coding.

These are design references, not a claim that the proposed plan is validated by those organizations.
